# best linear algebra student book


If your goal is **deep understanding** (rather than just passing an exam), these are the books most commonly recommended by university professors, researchers, and practitioners in AI, machine learning, and quantitative finance.

---

# 1. *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Introduction to Linear Algebra ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Author:** Gilbert Strang

### Best for

* Beginners
* Self-study
* AI / Data Science
* Engineering

### Why it's the best

This is arguably the most recommended linear algebra textbook in the world.

Instead of teaching matrix computations first, Strang emphasizes:

* Linear transformations
* Vector spaces
* Geometry
* Intuition
* Real-world applications

You'll understand **why** concepts work, not just **how** to compute them.

### Covers

* ✅ Vectors
* ✅ Matrix multiplication
* ✅ Linear transformations
* ✅ Rank
* ✅ Subspaces
* ✅ Basis
* ✅ Determinants
* ✅ Eigenvalues
* ✅ Orthogonality
* ✅ SVD
* ✅ PCA

**Difficulty:** ★★☆☆☆

---

# 2. *Linear Algebra Done Right* — Linear Algebra Done Right ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Author:** Sheldon Axler

### Best for

* Mathematics majors
* Theoretical understanding
* Graduate preparation

### Why it's famous

This book takes a completely different approach.

Instead of starting with determinants, Axler starts with

> **Vector spaces → Linear maps → Eigenvalues**

He even postpones determinants until much later because he believes they are not fundamental.

Many mathematicians consider this a more elegant way to learn linear algebra.

**Difficulty:** ★★★★☆

---

# 3. *Linear Algebra and Its Applications* — Linear Algebra and Its Applications


### Best for

* College courses
* Engineering students

Very student-friendly.

Includes:

* Numerous worked examples
* Practice problems
* Visual explanations

It is less theoretical than Axler and more structured than Strang.

**Difficulty:** ★★☆☆☆

# 4. *Linear Algebra* — Linear Algebra

This is a classic textbook.

Best for:

* Advanced mathematics
* Graduate study
* Pure mathematics

Many PhD students have learned from this book.

**Difficulty:** ★★★★★

# 5. *Matrix Analysis* — Matrix Analysis

This is considered one of the definitive references on matrices.

Topics include:

* Matrix theory
* Eigenvalues
* Positive definite matrices
* Matrix norms
* Spectral theory

Widely cited in research.

**Difficulty:** ★★★★★

---

# 6. *Numerical Linear Algebra* — Numerical Linear Algebra


Best for

* Machine Learning
* Scientific Computing
* Computer Science

Focuses on

* LU decomposition
* QR decomposition
* SVD
* Numerical stability
* Floating-point computation

This is the bridge between mathematics and practical computing.

**Difficulty:** ★★★★☆

---

# 7. *Matrix Computations* — Matrix Computations


One of the most influential books in scientific computing.

If you're writing numerical software or optimization libraries, this is an essential reference.

**Difficulty:** ★★★★★

---

# If your goal is AI

A great progression is:

1. **Gilbert Strang** → Build intuition.
2. **Numerical Linear Algebra** → Learn efficient algorithms.
3. **Matrix Analysis** → Deepen theoretical understanding.

This sequence covers almost everything you need for modern machine learning.

---

# If your goal is Quantitative Finance

Recommended order:

1. **Gilbert Strang**
2. **Matrix Analysis**
3. **Numerical Linear Algebra**

These topics are especially relevant:

* Covariance matrices
* Eigenvalue decomposition
* Positive semidefinite matrices
* PCA
* Least squares
* SVD

---

# If your goal is Mathematics

Recommended order:

1. **Linear Algebra Done Right**
2. **Hoffman & Kunze**
3. **Matrix Analysis**

This path develops a strong theoretical foundation before moving to advanced matrix theory.
